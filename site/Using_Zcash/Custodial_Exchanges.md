# <img src="/content-images/image-2024-02-03-173258092-a5440e5ee2.webp" alt="Custodial Exchanges icon" width="50"/>   Custodial Exchanges

## TL;DR

* A custodial exchange holds your ZEC and the private keys for it. You trade from an account balance, and the exchange sends funds out when you ask for a withdrawal.
* Most exchanges on this list handle transparent t-addresses only. Deposits and withdrawals on those addresses are public on the Zcash blockchain.
* Binance takes ZEC deposits through a [TEX address](/using-zcash/transparent-exchange-addresses), a transparent address that accepts transparent funds only.
* Withdraw your ZEC to a wallet you control and move it to a shielded address before you spend it.
* Each entry lists trading pairs, supported address types, typical deposit time and Ironwood status.

## Core Explanation

A custodial exchange keeps customer funds in wallets it controls. You open an account, usually pass an identity check, deposit ZEC, and trade from your balance. The exchange holds the private keys, so your coins move only when the exchange processes the request.

Ironwood is the shielded pool that arrived with the July 2026 network upgrade. Exchanges that only handle transparent t-addresses never touch it, so those are marked not applicable. Where an exchange does handle shielded addresses but has not said which pool it uses, the line says so rather than guessing.

## Visual / Analogy

A custodial exchange works like a coat check. You hand over your coat and get a ticket. The attendant keeps the coat on a rack you cannot reach, and you get it back when you show the ticket and the attendant brings it out. If the coat check closes for the night, you wait until it opens again.

Your account balance is the ticket. Your ZEC is the coat.

## Common Mistakes

- **Keeping ZEC on an exchange for the long term.** The exchange holds the keys. A hack, a withdrawal freeze, or a bankruptcy can lock your funds.
- **Spending straight from a transparent withdrawal address.** A transparent address is public. Shield the ZEC in your own wallet first.
- **Sending shielded funds to a TEX address with an older wallet.** A TEX address accepts transparent funds only. Wallets with ZIP 320 support move the amount through a temporary transparent address for you, so check your wallet before you send.
- **Skipping the Status line.** HTX lists ZEC deposits and withdrawals as suspended. Check the exchange's current status before you send funds.
