# NU6.2

> NU6.2 went live on Zcash mainnet at block 3,364,600 (June 3, 2026 UTC).

What you'll take away: what NU6.2 fixed, why Zcash paused shielded transactions for about a day, and how the network closed the hole without losing any funds.

NU6.2 is a Zcash [network upgrade](../start-here/network-upgrades), formally defined in [ZIP 257](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0257), that re-enabled the Orchard shielded protocol after an emergency pause. It corrected a soundness bug in Orchard's zero-knowledge circuit and restored [shielded](../using-zcash/shielded-pools) transactions. Think of it as the emergency-repair chapter of the Orchard bug story. The follow-up upgrade, [Ironwood (NU6.3)](../zcash-tech/ironwood), then added a new pool and a public audit of the old one.

Why this matters. Orchard is where most private Zcash payments happen. When a flaw was found in the math that keeps that pool honest, the network faced a hard choice: keep shielded transactions running with a known risk, or pause them until the fix was ready. NU6.2 is how Zcash chose safety first and still restored privacy within a day.

![NU6.2 emergency timeline: the Orchard bug was found on May 29, Orchard was paused by an emergency soft fork on June 2, and NU6.2 re-enabled it on a corrected circuit on June 3](/content-images/nu6-2-timeline-0b8de7fe26.webp)

## TL;DR

- NU6.2 activated on mainnet at block 3,364,600 (June 3, 2026 UTC), defined in ZIP 257.
- It re-enabled Orchard after an emergency pause: a soundness bug in Orchard's Action circuit had been found during a Shielded Labs audit and responsibly disclosed in late May 2026.
- An emergency soft fork on June 2, 2026 (zcashd v6.12.5, zebra v4.5.3) temporarily disabled Orchard transactions; transparent and Sapling kept working.
- NU6.2's hard fork fixed the circuit (added the missing constraint), produced a new verifying key that rejects old bad proofs, and made exact proof length a consensus rule.
- No funds were lost or frozen and there is no evidence of exploitation; auditing old-circuit value is Ironwood's (NU6.3) job, via a new pool and a public audit.

## Visual analogy

NU6.2 is the emergency-repair chapter of the Orchard story. Imagine a bank vault whose lock is found to have a subtle flaw: the bank stops using the vault for a day (the pause), installs a corrected lock (the new circuit and verifying key), and reopens. Everything stored before the repair is still there and untouched — but it will be audited and moved to a brand-new vault (Ironwood's new pool) under public supervision.


## What happened

The trigger was a soundness bug in Orchard's Action circuit, the math that is supposed to prove every shielded transaction obeys the rules. Independent researcher Taylor Hornby found it during a [Shielded Labs](../zcash-organizations/shielded-labs) audit and disclosed it responsibly in late May 2026. It had sat unnoticed since Orchard launched in 2022, and in theory it could let an invalid transaction pass as valid, which could allow funds to be stolen or double-spent inside the Orchard pool. Zcash's turnstile accounting still capped how much value could ever leave Orchard, so the total ZEC supply itself could not be inflated, but a flaw in the math that keeps shielded funds honest is serious, so the network acted within days. The [Ironwood](../zcash-tech/ironwood) page explains the bug and how it could create value in full.

## The emergency pause

On June 2, 2026, an emergency soft fork temporarily disabled all Orchard transactions. It shipped as a hotfix in zcashd v6.12.5 and zebra v4.5.3.

1. Shielded Orchard sends and receives were paused, so no new Orchard proofs could be created or accepted.
2. Transparent and Sapling transactions kept working normally.
3. No funds were moved, frozen, or lost. The pause only stopped new Orchard activity while the real fix was finished.

![What paused and what kept working: transparent and Sapling transactions ran the whole time, while only Orchard paused for about a day between June 2 and June 3](/content-images/nu6-2-what-paused-37bfa471d9.webp)

## What NU6.2 changed

The next day, June 3, 2026, the NU6.2 hard fork activated at block 3,364,600 and re-enabled Orchard on a corrected circuit.

![The NU6.2 circuit fix: the flawed circuit had a missing constraint and an old verifying key, and NU6.2 added the constraint to produce a corrected circuit with a new verifying key that rejects bad proofs](/content-images/nu6-2-the-fix-3038b4eb7e.webp)

1. It fixed the Action circuit's variable-base scalar multiplication gadget by adding the missing constraints.
2. That produced a new verifying key, so proofs made under the old, flawed circuit no longer validate, and every new proof must use the corrected one.
3. It made the exact proof length a consensus rule, closing a gap that was not enforced before.

The corrected code shipped in zcashd v6.20.0 and zebra v5.0.0, built on halo2_gadgets v0.5.0 and the orchard crate v0.14.0.

## What NU6.2 did not do

NU6.2 secured the circuit for every new transaction, but value created under the old rules still sat in the Orchard pool. Auditing that value and moving it to a clean pool is a separate job, and that is what [Ironwood (NU6.3)](../zcash-tech/ironwood) handles with its new pool and turnstile.

## Was anything lost

No. There is no evidence the bug was ever exploited, no unauthorized value creation, and no impact to user funds. Zcash's turnstile accounting confirmed the total supply stayed intact through the whole event.

## Common Mistakes

- **Believing shielded Zcash was down for a day.** Only Orchard paused; transparent and Sapling transactions kept working throughout.
- **Thinking the pause froze or destroyed funds.** No funds were moved, frozen, or lost — the pause only stopped new Orchard activity.
- **Assuming the bug let attackers print ZEC.** Turnstile accounting capped total value leaving Orchard, so the total ZEC supply could not be inflated; the risk was inside the pool, not to the supply.
- **Confusing the emergency soft fork (June 2) with NU6.2 (June 3).** The soft fork disabled Orchard; NU6.2 is the hard fork that fixed the circuit and re-enabled it.
- **Expecting NU6.2 to clean up old-circuit value.** NU6.2 secures every new transaction; auditing and migrating old value is Ironwood's separate job.


## Glossary

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| Shielded pool | The set of funds whose amounts and owners are hidden by zero-knowledge cryptography |
| Orchard | The shielded protocol introduced by NU5. It was Zcash's primary shielded pool at the time of NU6.2; Ironwood replaced it in NU6.3 and Orchard is now spend-only |
| Soundness bug | A flaw that lets an invalid transaction pass the proof check as if it were valid |
| Verifying key | The public value the network uses to check that a proof is valid. Correcting the circuit changed it |
| Soft fork | A backward-compatible rule change, one that paused Orchard without splitting the network |
| Hard fork | A coordinated rule change that all nodes must upgrade for, activated at a set block |

## FAQ

Did I lose access to my ZEC? No. For about a day, shielded Orchard transactions were paused, but no funds were lost or at risk, and transparent and Sapling transactions kept working.

Why pause instead of patching quietly? A soundness bug could let funds be stolen or double-spent inside the Orchard pool, so the safe move was to stop new Orchard transactions until the corrected circuit was live.

Do old shielded proofs still work? Proofs made under the old circuit no longer validate after NU6.2. Wallets on a supported release automatically use the corrected circuit.

Was the bug ever used? There is no evidence it was. It was caught by security research and fixed before any known harm.

How is NU6.2 different from Ironwood? NU6.2 corrected the circuit and re-enabled Orchard. [Ironwood (NU6.3)](../zcash-tech/ironwood) then added a new pool and a public turnstile to audit and move the old pool's value.
